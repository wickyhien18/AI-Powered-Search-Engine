Musicians are protesting to draw attention to political and humanitarian crises, to hold corporate leaders accountable, and to use their platform to mobilize public opinion. [1][4]

For example, a number of artists have joined the **No Music for Genocide** movement, calling for a boycott of Israeli streaming services in response to Israel’s military actions in Gaza and the suffering of Palestinian children. [3]

Independent musicians such as Macklemore and Annie Lennox have released pro‑Palestine songs and performed at the Grammys to demand an end to the Gaza conflict. [2]

Separately, a wave of artists—including Hotline TNT and King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard—have removed their catalogues from Spotify in protest of Spotify CEO Daniel Ek’s investments in defense companies, arguing that the platform’s financial ties conflict with their values. [5]

These protests illustrate how musicians use their art to challenge corporate and governmental policies and to advocate for social justice. [1][4]
